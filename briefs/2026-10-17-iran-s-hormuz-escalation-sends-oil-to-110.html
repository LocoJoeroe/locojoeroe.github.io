Iran's Hormuz Escalation Sends Oil to $110

Brent crude just crossed $110 a barrel. Iran is escalating attacks in the Strait of Hormuz — and every driver on earth is about to feel it.

The Strait of Hormuz is not abstract geography. Roughly 20% of the world's oil supply passes through it. When Iran starts shooting there, the entire market flinches — and prices spike instantly.

Meanwhile Ukraine is vowing to strike Russian refineries directly. Two separate oil-supply threats, hitting simultaneously. That's not a coincidence the market ignores.

The honest read: $110 isn't the ceiling if Hormuz traffic actually gets disrupted. Every 10% supply shock historically adds another $15-20 to the barrel — and that math flows straight to the pump within weeks.

The question isn't whether gas prices rise from here. It's how long Iran keeps the pressure on — and whether anyone can pull them back to the table fast enough to matter.
