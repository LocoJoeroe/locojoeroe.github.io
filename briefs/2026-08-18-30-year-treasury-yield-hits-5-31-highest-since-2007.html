30-Year Treasury Yield Hits 5.31% — Highest Since 2007

The 30-year Treasury yield just crossed 5.31% — the highest since 2007. Here's why that number touches almost everyone. 🧵

Treasury yields aren't abstract. The 30-year rate is the benchmark that mortgage rates, pension funds, and the US government's borrowing costs are all built on. When it rises, everything gets more expensive.

Mortgage rates are already brutal. A higher 30-year yield pushes them further — meaning the housing market, already frozen, gets colder. First-time buyers are the ones who feel it most.

The US is also sitting on a mountain of debt it needs to refinance. Every tick higher in yields means the government pays more in interest — billions more. That crowds out everything else in the budget.

Traders are watching FOMC minutes due this week for signals on where the Fed stands. But the bond market isn't waiting. It's already pricing in 'higher for longer' — and that bill is coming due for all of us.
