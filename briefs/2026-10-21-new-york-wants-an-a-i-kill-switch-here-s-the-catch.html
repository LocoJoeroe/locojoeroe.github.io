New York Wants an A.I. Kill Switch. Here's the Catch.

New York City held a hearing on October 5, 2026 where OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Meta testified under oath. Lawmakers were examining a package of AI proposals — including one that would require a human-operated shutdown capability and outside validation before any AI model is marketed, sold, or deployed in the city.

The proposal, T2026-2602, would have an independent third-party validator check performance, bias, privacy, and safety — and confirm the kill switch actually works. No validation or shutdown capability means a twenty-five-thousand-dollar penalty per instance. None of this is law yet as of October 6, 2026.

Here's the hard part: a shutdown can stop a company's running service. It can't stop people who already downloaded and are running their own copies. Civic tech group BetaNYC flagged this gap and asked the bill to specify exactly which operator controls which instance. That's the real question underneath the dramatic headline.
