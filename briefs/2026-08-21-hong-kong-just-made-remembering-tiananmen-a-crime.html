Hong Kong Just Made Remembering Tiananmen a Crime

Hong Kong just convicted the last people keeping Tiananmen's memory public — and the charge was 'inciting subversion.'

Lee Cheuk-yan, 69, and Chow Hang-tung, 41, organised the annual vigil marking China's 1989 crackdown. It was the only large public commemoration of Tiananmen left on Chinese territory.

A Hong Kong court found them guilty under Beijing's national security law, imposed in 2020. The UK Foreign Office condemned the convictions.

This isn't a crackdown on protest. It's a crackdown on memory. China has now criminalised the act of holding a candle for people killed by its own government.

The question isn't whether Hong Kong's autonomy is gone — it clearly is. The question is whether anyone outside Hong Kong is willing to pay a price to say so.
